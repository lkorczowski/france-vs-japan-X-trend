§03· What we found

Five lessons from the battlefield

    §04· Duels

    When an idea answers another

    A quote is a duel: someone takes a post and answers it with another idea. The ratio is the reach of the answer divided by the reach of the original. Above 1, the answer won.

    §05· Ranking

    Which ideas win their duels

    An Elo rating, as in chess. Every rebuttal is a match between two ideas. The idea whose post reached more people wins the match.

    §07· Origin

    How one cosplay started it

    1. 17 Oct

      High-school protests in France turn into riots and looting in several cities.

    2. 18 Oct

      A French post shows a Naruto cosplayer next to a burning car: "Obito cast a fire jutsu". 2.3M views.

    3. 18 Oct

      A Japanese account answers: stop soiling Japanese culture to do stupid things. 2.3M views.

    4. 18 Oct

      French answers: withdraw every anime inspired by Europe (5.1M views), and a list of Japanese "vices" that becomes the script of the jokes to come.

    5. 19 to 22 Oct

      Japanese X frames the riots as a Muslim and immigrant revolt. A minority answers with data: most rioters are students born in France.

    6. 21 to 25 Oct

      The French joke wave: Vegeta is Algerian (17.9M), sperm and sex dolls (11.5M), the 65-hour week (10.1M). X auto-translates every one of them, and the English-speaking world watches.

    §08· Method

    How this was measured

    1. Corpus. 59 seed posts (every tracked post above 500K views, plus posts found by hand), 58 posts found by searching X in eight languages (500K views and more, 250K for third parties), 8 posts they quote, 2 origin posts seen in screenshots, and 115 quotes read in the Quotes tab of the 15 most viewed posts. 240 posts, about 330M views. Six viral posts that mention France or Japan without the conflict were excluded.
    2. Multimodal reading. Every post was read with its image or video frame, because the joke often lives in the picture. Each post was annotated with one idea, written as a neutral sentence that states the implied meaning, the camp it serves, the community that posted it and its register.
    3. Concept induction. The 21 concepts were induced from the posts, not fixed in advance, following concept induction with language models (LLooM). A concept is contested ground: both camps can hold ideas inside it.
    4. Map. Ideas are embedded with a sentence model (all-mpnet-base-v2). Concept centroids are placed by metric MDS on cosine distance, like the intertopic map of pyLDAvis. Battle lines place each concept by the share of its views serving each camp.
    5. Can it be automated?
    6. Limits. Seed posts were partly chosen by hand. Views measure reach, not agreement. The camp of a post is a reading, never an assumption about the origin of a person. Post text is never displayed: ideas are paraphrased, and insults are counted but not linked.
